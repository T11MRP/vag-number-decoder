Origin, not fitment

The type code says where a part started life, not what it fits. A 1K0 part covers most of PQ35: Golf Mk5 and Mk6, Golf Plus, Jetta, Touran, Caddy, Tiguan Mk1, Passat B6, Audi A3 8P, TT 8J, Octavia Mk2, Leon Mk2, Altea. Only the catalogue tells you the real fitment.

The four that cost money

Left means passenger on a UK car

Odd last digit is left, even is right, as seen from a left hand drive driver's seat. So on a British car, left is the passenger side. When a customer says "driver's side", ask which side of the car they actually mean before you pick anything.

Model year is not plate year

A car registered September 2019 on a 69 plate is often a 2020 model year car. VAG parts break on model year. Take the VIN.

Never quote before you have a number

Get the VIN, find the part, confirm stock, then tell the customer what you have. Saying "yes I've got one" before you have checked is how wrong parts get posted and how the 4.9 rating slips.

A revision can change fitment

1K0615301 and 1K0615301AA are not automatically interchangeable. Check rather than assume the later letter supersedes.

House listing format

Title
Brand + models + year range + component + part number
Example
Audi A6 S6 RS6 2005-2011 front lower wishbone (left) 4F0407151A
SKU
The part number exactly, colour suffix included
Vendor
Partshaus Ltd
Tags
_Golf _Tiguan _A6 — underscore prefixed model names, one per fitted model
_NOPIC
Tag meaning no photograph yet. A work queue, not a permanent state
Variants
One per product. One part number, one product

List every model the part fits in the title, not just the one the type code came from. That title is what search engines match when somebody googles a bare part number, which is where most of the traffic comes from.
